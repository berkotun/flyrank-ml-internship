Predicting SEO Content Decay: A Machine Learning Approach to Identify Underperforming Search Assets

Berk Ötün · Machine Learning Intern, FlyRank AI Internship 2026

Abstract

Identifying content that underperforms in Google Search is critical for maintaining digital visibility. This work asks whether a machine learning model can predict which pages need an SEO refresh more reliably than a static heuristic rule. Using a Random Forest classifier trained on Google Search Console metrics, decaying content was modelled and validated with a client-grouped split for honest evaluation. The model outperformed the rule-based baseline, reaching an F1-score of 0.983 on unseen clients. The result is a directional decision-support tool that helps SEO teams prioritise manual content audits.

1. Question

How can "high-rank, low-click" decaying content be identified across large numbers of URLs without relying on rigid static rules? The goal is a directional decision-support tool that helps marketing teams prioritise their content refresh pipeline instead of auditing pages manually.

2. Data

  • Source: the March 2026 release of the fact_content_daily_performance table from the FlyRank internship data warehouse.
  • Scope: daily Google Search Console performance metrics across multiple clients, aggregated per client and content ID.
  • Exclusions: personally identifiable information, client names and raw search queries were excluded to keep the work public-safe.

3. Methodology

  • Label: a page is flagged as problematic when it has more than 100 impressions and an average position of 20 or better, yet a CTR below 1%.
  • Features: gsc_impressions, gsc_avg_position, content_age_days.
  • Leakage control: gsc_clicks was dropped explicitly because it feeds the CTR formula behind the label; feature importance confirmed no single feature carried more than 95% of the weight.
  • Baseline: the Week-4 heuristic rule (position <= 10 and CTR < 0.01).
  • Validation: GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id, so the model is scored only on client domains it never saw during training.

5. Limitations

  • Directional only. The model gives a directional indicator of decay measured purely on Search Console data; it has no view of business conversions or backlink profiles.
  • No auto-execution. A low score does not mean the page is bad — it may be a navigational brand page. Outputs are decision-support signals, not automated triggers.
  • No claim is made about Google's ranking algorithm or causal refresh impact.

6. Ranked recommendations

  • Priority 1 — Content refresh (R-01): high impressions (>500), average position <= 15, CTR < 0.01 → send to editorial for metadata and title rewrites.
  • Priority 2 — Technical SEO audit (R-02): ranking in the top three but not earning clicks → investigate SERP layout, zero-click intent or technical issues.
  • Priority 3 — Prune / consolidate (R-03): stale content older than 365 days with zero clicks → evaluate for a 301 redirect.
  • No-go list: homepage, legal pages and core pricing pages are excluded from automated pruning; page deletions are never automated on this score.
