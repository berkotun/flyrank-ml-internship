Predicting SEO Content Decay: A Machine Learning Approach to Identify Underperforming Search Assets

Berk Ötün · Machine Learning Intern, FlyRank AI Internship 2026

Abstract

Finding pages that still rank but no longer earn clicks is one of the slower jobs in SEO, and it usually gets done by hand. I wanted to know whether a model could do that triage better than the fixed rule I had been using earlier in the internship. I trained a Random Forest classifier on Google Search Console metrics and scored it with a client-grouped split, so it was always tested on domains it had never seen during training. It reached an F1 score of 0.983, against 0.803 for the rule. What comes out of it is a prioritization signal for a content audit queue, not a verdict on any single page.

1. Question

Across a few thousand URLs, which ones rank well but no longer earn the clicks that ranking should bring? Checking them one by one does not scale. A fixed threshold works until the client mix changes, and then it quietly stops working without anyone noticing. So I framed the problem as binary classification and let the ranking of candidates come from the data instead of from a number I picked myself.

2. Data

  • Source. The March 2026 release of the fact_content_daily_performance table from the FlyRank internship data warehouse.
  • Scope. Daily Google Search Console performance metrics for several clients, aggregated per client and content ID.
  • Exclusions. I stripped out personally identifiable information, client names and raw search queries so this write-up stays safe to publish.

3. Methodology

  • Label. A page counts as problematic when it has more than 100 impressions and an average position of 20 or better, but a CTR below 1%.
  • Features. gsc_impressions, gsc_avg_position, content_age_days.
  • Leakage control. I dropped gsc_clicks on purpose, because it sits inside the CTR formula that defines the label. I checked feature importance afterwards and no single feature carried more than 95% of the weight.
  • Baseline. The heuristic rule I wrote in week four: position <= 10 and CTR < 0.01.
  • Validation. GroupShuffleSplit on client_hash_id. The model is only ever scored on client domains it did not train on.

The whole difference sits in recall. The rule is never wrong when it fires, which is why its precision is 1.000, but it misses roughly a third of the decaying pages. The model finds those, and it finds them on client domains it had not seen during training. That last part is what I cared about most, because a model that only works on the clients it memorized is not much use to anyone.

5. Limitations

  • Directional only. Everything here is measured on Search Console data. There is no view of conversions, revenue or backlinks, so a low score is a reason to look at a page, not a reason to act on it.
  • No automatic execution. A flagged page is not automatically a weak page. It may be a navigational brand page that behaves this way by design. The output feeds a human review queue and nothing else.
  • No causal claims. I make no claim about how Google ranks pages, and none about the causal effect of refreshing content.

6. Ranked recommendations

  • Priority 1, content refresh (R-01). More than 500 impressions, average position 15 or better, CTR below 1%. These go to editorial for title and metadata rewrites.
  • Priority 2, technical SEO audit (R-02). Ranking in the top three and still not earning clicks. Worth checking SERP layout, zero-click intent and technical issues before anyone touches the copy.
  • Priority 3, prune or consolidate (R-03). Older than 365 days with no clicks at all. Evaluate for a 301 redirect.
  • No-go list. The homepage, legal pages and core pricing pages stay out of any automated pruning. Nothing gets deleted on the basis of this score.

7. Reproducibility

The notebooks and the full pipeline are in the public repository at github.com/berkotun/flyrank-ml-internship. The capstone notebook is work/notebooks/capstone.ipynb and the weekly assignment notebooks sit in the same folder. Seeds are fixed at random_state=42, so a fresh clone reproduces the numbers reported above.
